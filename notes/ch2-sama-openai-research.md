# Chapter 2 hook: the Kenyan workers behind ChatGPT's safety filter

Research notes for the Demystifying AI chapter. Nothing here is in the book yet.
Searches were run in November 2026. Check each point against the source before
using it.

## Primary source

TIME, "Exclusive: OpenAI Used Kenyan Workers on Less Than $2 Per Hour to Make
ChatGPT Less Toxic" (Billy Perrigo, 18 January 2023).
https://time.com/6247678/openai-chatgpt-kenya-workers/
Read in full on 25 November 2026. The page opens with a content warning.

## The story, as TIME reports it

- **Why:** ChatGPT's predecessor, GPT-3, was trained on hundreds of billions of
  words from the internet and was prone to violent, sexist and racist output.
  OpenAI's fix was a detector trained on labelled examples of toxic text, to
  filter output before users saw it and to clean future training data.
- **Who:** OpenAI sent tens of thousands of text snippets, from November 2021,
  to Sama, a San Francisco firm with workers in Kenya, Uganda and India. Sama
  markets itself as an "ethical AI" company. The workers were in Nairobi.
- **What:** About three dozen workers, in three teams (sexual abuse, hate
  speech, violence), read and labelled graphic text, including child sexual
  abuse, bestiality, murder, suicide, torture, self-harm and incest.
- **Volume:** Workers said 150 to 250 passages per nine-hour shift. Sama says it
  was 70 per shift.
- **Pay:** TIME reports take-home pay of about $1.32 to $2 an hour depending on
  seniority and performance. Junior labellers had a basic salary of KES 21,000
  a month plus a bonus of about $70 for the explicit content. Sama says workers
  earned $1.46 to $3.74 an hour after tax. OpenAI paid Sama $12.50 an hour under
  the contracts, which TIME says was six to nine times what workers took home;
  Sama says the $12.50 covers all its costs (infrastructure, salaries, benefits).
  TIME gives the Nairobi minimum wage for a receptionist at the time as $1.52
  an hour.
- **Support:** All four employees TIME interviewed said they were mentally
  scarred and that the "wellness" sessions were rare and unhelpful. Sama says
  individual and group sessions with licensed therapists were available at any
  time. OpenAI says it issued no productivity targets and that Sama handled pay
  and mental health provision.
- **Money:** Contracts were worth about $200,000 on paper. Both companies say
  that wasn't paid in full. OpenAI says about $150,000.
- **The images project:** In February 2022 Sama began a pilot collecting sexual
  and violent images for OpenAI. Sama delivered a sample of 1,400 images; OpenAI
  paid $787.50. Some were in categories OpenAI labels C4 (child sexual abuse).
  OpenAI says it never intended to collect that content. Sama ended the pilot,
  and cancelled the remaining OpenAI work. Sama says it fired the people who
  handled the request without proper vetting.
- **The ending:** Sama's last batch went to OpenAI in March 2022, eight months
  early. Workers say managers told them it followed TIME's 14 February 2022
  story about Sama's Facebook moderators. Most of the workers moved to
  lower-paid work without the bonus; some lost their jobs. On 10 January 2023
  Sama announced it would exit all natural language processing and content
  moderation work, including a $3.9 million Facebook contract, costing about 200
  jobs in Nairobi.
- **Named workers:** None. All four spoke anonymously.

## What happened afterwards

- **July 2023, petition to Parliament:** Workers who helped remove harmful
  content from ChatGPT petitioned Kenya's lawmakers to investigate the nature,
  conditions and operations of Big Tech firms that outsource through firms like
  Sama, and to legislate for outsourced harmful technology work (TechCrunch, 14
  July 2023; ITWeb Africa, 18 July 2023). They said they got no psychosocial
  support and developed PTSD, paranoia, depression, anxiety and insomnia
  (ITWeb). Foxglove (UK non-profit) backed them, with the content moderators'
  union. Sama said it welcomed third-party audits. OpenAI called the work
  "challenging" and said it sets ethical and wellness standards; ITWeb says it
  didn't respond to the specific PTSD claims.
  - https://techcrunch.com/2023/07/14/workers-that-made-chatgpt-less-harmful-ask-lawmakers-to-stem-alleged-exploitation-by-big-tech
  - https://itweb.africa/article/exclusive-openai-responds-to-kenyas-toxic-work-petition/lwrKxq3YNky7mg1o
- **2026, Data Labelers Association:** 404 Media (12 March 2026, partly
  paywalled) describes a Kenyan Data Labelers Association, with Michael Geoffrey
  Asia as secretary general, demanding better pay, mental health services, an
  end to "draconian" non-disclosure agreements and better benefits. It held a
  large sign-up event in Nairobi in February 2026.
  https://www.404media.co/ai-is-african-intelligence-the-workers-who-train-ai-are-fighting-back/
- **Court cases:** A search result says litigation over pay, mental health
  support and union rights has run in Kenyan courts for over three years, with a
  ruling postponed as late as February 2026. Not read, so unverified.

## Related but different (do not mix up)

The Meta moderators' case in Kenya is about Facebook content moderation, not
ChatGPT. Sama was Meta's contractor. Reported steps: the Employment and Labour
Relations Court ruled in 2023 that Meta can be sued in Kenya; the Court of
Appeal dismissed Meta's appeals (reported September 2024); a report dated about
April 2026 says the court ruled in favour of 187 former moderators. Only search
snippets seen. Good material for Chapter 3 (Illusion of Autonomy), but read the
rulings before using it.

## Suggested use in the chapter

The hook is the question "how does ChatGPT know not to say terrible things?"
The answer is people, in Nairobi, reading the worst of the internet first. That
shows AI isn't magic: it's data plus human judgment, and someone pays a cost for
that judgment. Then move on to how learning with human feedback works
(postcode sorting, from the podcast).
